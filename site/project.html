Final Project

Statistical Evaluation of an AI System

Teams of four will evaluate an AI system or an AI evaluation protocol. The goal is not to produce the flashiest model; it is to make a defensible statistical claim.

Shared Requirements

Both tracks use the same statistical habits. Students should be able to explain each design choice and defend the recommendation under questioning.

Own The Baseline

Build, explain, evaluate, and defend a simple baseline model as a real comparison point.

Quantify Trust

Use uncertainty or calibration evidence to show what the performance result can support.

Stress The Claim

Use robustness, ablation, or sensitivity analysis to test whether the conclusion survives.

Project Tracks

The course supports four choices equally: two industry-focused ShopSmart options and two research-focused Judging the Judge options.

Industry Track

ShopSmart Food Catalog

Evaluate whether an image classifier is reliable enough for food-related marketplace category suggestions. The focus is deployment evidence, guardrails, confidence, and error consequences.

Food-101 subset image baseline confidence thresholds

Industry Track

ShopSmart Pet Catalog

Evaluate fine-grained pet image classification where visually similar categories make top-1 accuracy incomplete. Students may compare coarse and fine labels.

Oxford-IIIT Pets subset coarse vs. fine labels error severity

Research Track

Judging Human Preferences

Evaluate whether simple baselines or an LLM judge can predict human preference labels, and critique what those labels actually measure.

SHP subset length bias LLM judge

Research Track

Judging Instruction Following

Compare deterministic rule checks, simple baselines, and LLM-as-judge scores on constrained instruction-following tasks.

IFEval-style subset rule checker judge prompt ablation

Required Components

Each team chooses a specific evaluation question within its track.

Analysis

  • Scenario framing and target population
  • Dataset and benchmark EDA
  • Baseline model ownership
  • AI system or judge evaluation
  • Uncertainty or calibration evidence
  • Robustness, ablation, or sensitivity analysis

Reflection

  • Evidence-backed recommendation
  • Limitations and what would change the conclusion
  • Optional human evaluation protocol
  • Meta-evaluation of AI tools used in the analysis
  • Clear account of what the team verified independently

Deliverables

Checkpoints are the main grade because they show process, judgment, and revision.

Week 9 · Checkpoint 1

Team, option, model/dataset, evaluation questions, initial EDA, benchmark critique, robustness or ablation plan.

Week 11 · Checkpoint 2

Baseline model, AI results, model comparison, uncertainty or calibration evidence, first client-facing interpretation.

Week 13 · Ending Artifact

Robustness/ablation, draft recommendation, draft report structure, and video outline.

Sunday Before Week 14 · Video Due

Five-minute recorded video due at 11:59pm, no exceptions, so the instructor can prepare oral discussion questions.

Week 14 · Oral Discussion and Report

Short accountability discussion plus concise written report recording the final analysis.
